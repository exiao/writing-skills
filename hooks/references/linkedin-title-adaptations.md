# LinkedIn Title Adaptations

Two different games on one platform. Decide which you are playing before writing the title.

## Feed post (written for humans scrolling)

- Slide 1 of carousels = the hook (bold text, minimal design)
- No third-party links in body (suppresses reach) — put in first comment
- First 2 lines visible before "see more" — these ARE your hook
- Suspense works here. Withholding the answer is what earns the "see more" tap.

## Pulse article (written to rank in Google and get quoted by AI)

Different rules, and they are close to the opposite of the feed rules.

- **Put the exact search query in the headline, in the first 50 characters.** This is the
  single strongest ranking signal measured. Query fully present in headline: 42.1% rank
  rate. Query absent: 7.2%. Query inside the first 50 chars: 29.1%; at 50 to 150 chars:
  16.3%.
- **Answer in the first line.** AI Overviews and Perplexity quote from the opening of the
  page. If the first 200 characters are setup, there is nothing to quote, even when the
  answer sits 400 words down. Lead with the answer plus one number, then tell the story.
- **The slug locks on publish.** LinkedIn builds the URL, title tag, and meta description
  from the headline at the moment you hit publish, and the slug can never be changed. Save
  a draft, look at the URL, fix the headline, then publish. Thirty seconds, one chance.
- Hook craft does not measurably help a Pulse article rank. Word count, heading count,
  paragraph length, lists, and emoji showed no effect. Write well for the reader, but do
  not expect style to move rankings.

Source: 1,260 searches and 9,805 LinkedIn URLs analysed, @borjafat, Aug 2026. Observational
study, US desktop, English. Percentages compare indexed pages that ranked against indexed
pages that did not, so treat them as group differences rather than a forecast for one post.

## The conflict, resolved

Feed wants suspense. Search wants the answer up front. You cannot serve both in one title.

Pick the goal first. If the post is meant to compound in search for a year, write the Pulse
headline and accept a flatter feed performance. If it is meant to land this week, write the
feed hook and accept that it will not rank.
